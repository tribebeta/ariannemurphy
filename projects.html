ADA Web Compliance
Communications Initiative

County of Riverside Executive Office · 2025–2026
01

Following the Department of Justice’s April 2024 final rule updating Title II of the Americans with Disabilities Act, all state and local governments serving populations of 50,000 or more are required to meet WCAG 2.1 Level AA web accessibility standards by April 24, 2026. For Riverside County — serving 2.5 million residents across 40 departments — this required an immediate, large-scale communications and coordination effort.

Arianne leads the countywide communications strategy for this initiative, serving as the central communications bridge between the Riverside County IT Department and communicators across all 40 county departments. The county’s IT team is implementing DocAccess, an AI-powered remediation tool that automatically brings PDFs into ADA-compliant format, and Arianne is responsible for ensuring every department understands the tool, the timeline, and their individual responsibilities before the federal deadline.

Leading communications coordination across 40 county departments to drive adoption of the DocAccess AI remediation tool ahead of the April 24, 2026 federal compliance deadline
Using Microsoft Copilot to generate automated, personalized department-level updates that communicate implementation progress and step-by-step guidance — keeping 40 teams informed at scale without one-on-one outreach for every update
Translating complex IT and legal compliance language into clear, actionable communications that department staff can understand and act on immediately
Coordinating countywide document review process in which departments categorize all public-facing PDFs as Archive or Remediate, enabling IT to prioritize the remediation queue efficiently
Partnering with IT to distribute a step-by-step DocAccess implementation guide ensuring seamless integration with each department’s existing website infrastructure
Managing communications that directly impact public accessibility for 2.5 million Riverside County residents — including people with visual, auditory, motor, and cognitive disabilities
